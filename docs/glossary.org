feels heavier than it needs to be.  If a definition below still feels
technical, that is the page's fault, not yours.

* The short version

Three words carry most of the player:

- A *backend* is where your music lives and how orpheus reaches it.
- The *queue* is what will play next.
- A *tab* is one view of your music, such as the queue, a folder or a search.

Everything else is a detail around those three.

* The player and its parts

** Backend
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: backend
:END:

The part of orpheus that knows how to reach your music.  Three ship here:
~mpd~ talks to a Music Player Daemon, ~local~ plays files and links on this
machine, and ~ssh~ copies files from another machine before playing them
here.  The player looks the same with all three.  Press ~B~ to switch.

** Client and daemon
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: client-and-daemon
:END:

A *daemon* is a program that keeps running in the background.  MPD is a
daemon: it owns the music library and often the audio output.  orpheus is the
*client* that asks it to play, pause, queue and browse.  The local and SSH
backends do not need a daemon; they start playback themselves.

** Library
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: library
:END:

The collection of music a backend can see.  For MPD it is the daemon's
configured music directory.  For the local backend it is
~orpheus-local-directory~.  For SSH it is the remote directory named by
~orpheus-ssh-directory~.  orpheus does not move or rename your files.

** Queue
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: queue
:END:

The list of tracks that will play next.  MPD keeps its queue inside the
daemon; the local and SSH backends keep theirs in Emacs.  The Queue tab shows
the queue, and ~D~ clears it.

** Now playing
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: now-playing
:END:

The track that is playing right now, shown in the card above the list and in
the header line.  The card's controls are ordinary buttons, so the mouse and
the keyboard reach the same commands.

** Tab
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: tab
:END:

One view of your music.  The number keys choose a tab, ~TAB~ moves to the next
one, and each tab remembers where you were.  A backend may offer a different
set of tabs depending on what it can do.

** Browse
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: browse
:END:

Moving through folders, artists or albums instead of playing a single row.
~RET~ enters the row at point, and ~^~ goes back up one level.

** Mark
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: mark
:END:

A temporary selection of one or more rows.  ~m~ marks a row, ~u~ unmarks it,
and ~a~ queues the marked rows.  Marks are a convenience, not a saved change.

** Portable playlist
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: portable-playlist
:END:

A playlist stored as an M3U8 file instead of inside a backend.  It can be
opened by another backend when the files are available there.  ~N~ creates
one, ~e~ edits one, and ~C-x C-s~ saves the queue as one.

** Cache
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: cache
:END:

A local copy of something fetched earlier, usually a track or cover from
SSH.  The cache makes a replay instant and survives a restart.  It is safe to
delete; orpheus fetches the file again when it needs it.

** Transfer
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: transfer
:END:

A file being copied from another machine.  The Downloads tab shows what is
queued, moving or finished.  ~d~ cancels a transfer and ~RET~ plays or retries
one.

** Multiplexing
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: multiplexing
:END:

A way for SSH to reuse one connection for several small requests.  It saves a
fresh login for each folder change.  A *control socket* is the little local
file SSH uses to coordinate that shared connection.  If the path is too long
or the host refuses it, orpheus retries without multiplexing so browsing
still works.

** Equalizer
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: equalizer
:END:

A set of volume adjustments for frequency bands, applied by mpv.  It works
with the local and SSH backends.  MPD sends its audio straight to the
daemon's output, so orpheus cannot filter it.  The menu on ~E~ is its simple
mode; the bar graph on ~M-E~ is the advanced one.

** Lyrics
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: lyrics
:END:

The words of a track, shown by ~L~.  A timed file is called LRC: its lines
start with a time such as ~[01:12.45]~, which is what lets orpheus mark the
line that is playing.  A plain text file has no times and is shown as it is.
Old files keep the words inside themselves instead of beside them, and each
backend reads whichever of those it can reach.

** Visualizer
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: visualizer
:END:

The spectrum drawing at the bottom of the player.  It listens to what the
machine is playing through cava, so it is not tied to one backend.

** Command menu
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: command-menu
:END:

The menu opened by ~?~.  It groups commands by what they do and shows the key
next to each one.  You can click an entry instead of remembering the key.

** Mode line
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: mode-line
:END:

The line at the bottom of an Emacs window.  ~orpheus-mode-line-mode~ can show
the current track there, with clickable controls, without opening the player.

** Init file
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: init-file
:END:

Your Emacs startup file, usually =~/.emacs.d/init.el= or
=~/.config/emacs/init.el=.  The examples in this guide are meant to go there,
or to be evaluated once with ~M-x eval-buffer~ while you experiment.

** Option
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: option
:END:

A named setting, such as ~orpheus-backend~.  You can set one with ~setq~ in
your init file or change it with ~M-x customize-group RET orpheus RET~.  The
[[file:configuration.org][Configuration]] page lists every option.

** nil and t
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: nil-and-t
:END:

Emacs Lisp's false and true values.  ~nil~ means off, empty or “not set”; ~t~
means on.  A table that says ~nil~ usually means “use the sensible default”.

* Keyboard words
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: keyboard-words
:END:


- ~M-x~ means hold Alt and press x.  In a terminal where Alt does not work,
  press Escape, release it, then press x.
- ~RET~ is the Enter or Return key.
- ~C-x~ means hold Control and press x.
- ~S-TAB~ means Shift and Tab together.
- A *command* is something you can run with ~M-x~ or bind to a key.  Every
  button in orpheus runs one.

If these are new, the [[file:usage.org#first-five-minutes][first five minutes]] section shows them in context.
